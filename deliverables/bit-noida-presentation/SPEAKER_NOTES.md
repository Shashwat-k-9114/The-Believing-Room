# The Believing Room at BIT Noida: speaker notes
Target: 6 to 8 minutes. Roughly 45 to 60 seconds a slide, plus pauses.

## 1. The opening
- Walk on while the opening plays; it runs by itself, about six seconds. Let the logo land before you speak.
- Open warmly: "Good morning. We're the founders of The Believing Room, and this is our college."
- Read the three lines slowly: Your Talent. Your Stage. Your Opportunity.
- Tell them what the next few minutes are: what we're building, why it exists, and how you can be part of it. Keep it under a minute.

## 2. The hook
- Start with a question: how many of you have sent a résumé and heard nothing back?
- Click once. The gold strike crosses the résumé and three things appear that no résumé holds.
- Say them, then personalise: the app you built at 2 a.m., how you explain things to friends, the idea you keep coming back to.
- Land the point: a page can't hold all of that. We think the people who hire should get to meet it.
- Do not criticise recruiters or claim anything about how hiring works everywhere. This is about how it feels from a student's side.

## 3. Introducing The Believing Room
- The doors open on the stage. Pause on the headline: what if your next opportunity began with your story?
- Click once; the scene builds. The candidate is in the light, the panel sits in front, the audience watches.
- Explain it in plain words: a live pitch show. You present what you can do, in your own words, and recruiters on the panel question you. It is filmed.
- If someone asks, mention that the format is inspired by the energy of live entrepreneurial pitching, adapted for students and recruitment.

## 4. 16 recruiters
- This is the moment. Before you click, say: here is what we've been building. Then click once and stay quiet for the reveal; it takes about five seconds.
- Sixteen recruiters secured. Say the number, then the line: real recruiters, real conversations, real possibilities.
- Be precise about what it means: sixteen recruiters have come on board for the show. Each recruiter makes their own decisions.
- Do not say sixteen companies, sixteen internships or sixteen jobs, and do not promise offers. We have not named the recruiters on this slide because we only name them once confirmed.

## 5. Four paths
- Click once and the four paths rise in turn.
- Technology: show them how you build. Finance: make the numbers speak. Sales and marketing: sell the idea, starting with yourself. Animation and multimedia: let the work move them.
- Key message: talent comes in more than one shape. You do not need a perfect transcript; you need something real to show.
- Invite them to find themselves in one of the four. Do not suggest that every path has openings, or promise any particular number of roles.

## 6. How the journey works
- Two clicks. The first draws the journey up to the pitch; the second finishes it.
- Apply: tell us what you can do. Shortlist: our team reviews applications. Pitch: you present live and take the panel's questions.
- Second click: recruiters evaluate, and each recruiter decides for themselves. Potential opportunity: possibly an internship or an early-career role.
- Read the line at the bottom aloud. Being shortlisted does not guarantee a slot, and no outcome is guaranteed. Honesty here builds trust.

## 7. What students gain
- Click once; the five lines rise one after another.
- Frame it bigger than an internship. A stage to show what you can do. Recruiters who see it first-hand. Practice presenting ideas under real questions. Feedback and connections worth keeping. And a possible path to an internship or early-career role.
- The line to remember: the point is to be seen, not just screened.
- These are things we are building toward; experiences vary by candidate and none is guaranteed. Say so in your own words.

## 8. Back to where it began
- Slow down. The first line appears by itself; click for the second.
- Tell the real story in two or three sentences: you sat in these seats, here is what you wished existed, and this is what you are building.
- Keep it warm and short: it started with students like us; now we're building a stage for students like you.
- We are here as alumni sharing something we are building. Do not describe BIT Noida as a partner, sponsor or endorser.

## 9. The finale
- The slide builds itself and then holds. Leave it up for the rest of the session.
- Say it: your story deserves an audience. Then the one ask: scan the code and apply to pitch.
- The code and the button both open the candidate application page. Give people a few quiet seconds to scan; mention the address on screen for anyone who prefers typing.
- Close with thanks and take questions with this frame still projected.
